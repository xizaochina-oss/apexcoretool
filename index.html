4. Technical FAQ & Troubleshooting

Q1: Why do diamond saw blades wobble or burn when cutting reinforced concrete in high-temperature regions (e.g., Middle East/Southeast Asia)?
Answer: Wobbling or segment burning typically occurs due to core overheating and inappropriate bond hardness. In high ambient temperatures, wet cooling must be maintained. If dry cutting, choose an ACT blade with laser-welded segments and heat-sink expansion slots to prevent steel core warping under extreme thermal stress.
Q2: How do I choose between different arbor sizes (22.23mm vs 25.4mm vs 50mm) and thread adapters (M14 vs 1-1/4"-7 UNC) for European vs North American power tools?
Answer: European power tools commonly use 22.23mm arbors for angle grinders and M14 threads for core drills. North American standards frequently utilize 5/8"-11 threads and 1-1/4"-7 UNC fittings. ACT supplies standard reduction rings and multi-fit brass/steel thread conversion adapters to guarantee 100% compatibility across Bosch, Makita, Hilti, and DeWalt machines worldwide.
Q3: Why are my diamond core drill bits glazing over or failing to cut through ultra-hard granite or 5000+ PSI rebar concrete?
Answer: When cutting hard non-abrasive materials, a bond matrix that is too hard will prevent the diamond grit from exposing new sharp edges (glazing). Solution: Use an ACT soft-matrix diamond bit designed for hard stone/rebar, or briefly dress the glazed bit by drilling into highly abrasive material (e.g., sandstone or cinder block) to reveal fresh synthetic diamond particles.
Q4: What is the difference between Laser-Welded segments and High-Frequency Silver-Brazed segments for industrial road saws?
Answer: Silver-brazed segments rely on solder melting points and are strictly intended for wet cutting. Laser-welded segments physically fuse the diamond segment to the steel core, providing maximum joint strength. For dry cutting or heavy-duty road and wall sawing where water supply may fail, ACT strongly recommends laser-welded blades for operator safety and longevity.
