Module 1: Product Science & Selection Guide

Understanding Key Drivers of Blade Performance, Lifespan, and Application Specifics

1.1 Performance & Lifespan Drivers

Diamond Grade & Grit

High-purity single-crystal diamonds provide impact resistance. Coarser grit delivers fast cutting; finer grit ensures clean, chip-free edge finishing.

Bond Matrix Formulation

Metal bond alloy (Cobalt, Bronze, Iron) balances wear rate. Principle: Soft bonds for hard materials; Hard bonds for abrasive soft materials.

Welding Technology

Laser Welded: Maximum heat resistance, essential for heavy dry cutting.
High-Frequency: High cost-efficiency for wet cutting.

1.2 Comprehensive Blade & Bit Category Selector

Asphalt & Green Concrete

Equipped with hard matrix and undercut protection segments to withstand severe abrasive slurry wear.

Reinforced Concrete

High-cobalt bond matrix engineered to slice through dense rebar grids with maximum segment retention.

Granite & Hard Stone

Narrow keyhole or silent core design engineered for fast penetration and low noise output.

Quartz & Sintered Stone

Ultra-fine mesh diamonds designed to prevent chipping on high-value, brittle countertop surfaces.

Marble & Soft Stone

Electroplated or soft sintered bonds operating at lower friction temperatures to preserve stone color.

Wet Diamond Core Bits

Heavy-duty steel barrels designed for structural coring, highway guardrails, and utility piping.

Dry Drilling Core Bits

Engineered with special side dust slot channels for indoor electrical and plumbing installations without water.

Wall Saw & Diamond Wire

Industrial demolition grade tools engineered for high tensile load cutting on mass concrete structures.

TCT Wood & Multi-Purpose

Tungsten Carbide Tipped blades designed for timber, construction formwork, and nail-embedded wood.

Module 2: Field Maintenance & Technical Troubleshooting

10 Standardized Operating Procedures: Problem, Root Cause & Field Solution

1

Cutting Speed Drops / Blade Glazing

Root Cause: Hard matrix bond glazed over diamond particles.
Field Solution: Cut into highly abrasive sandstone or firebrick 3-5 times to strip the bond and re-expose sharp diamond grits (Self-Sharpening).

2

Overheating & Segment Loss

Root Cause: Intermittent cooling water or severe dry thermal shock.
Field Solution: Maintain continuous high-pressure water flow on the contact zone. Switch to Laser Welded blades for heavy dry cutting.

3

Blade Wobbling & Arbor Wear

Root Cause: RPM mismatch, loose flanges, or worn spindle.
Field Solution: Match machine RPM strictly to blade specs. Inspect and clean flanges; replace worn bushings before mounting.

4

Core Bit Jamming in Rebar

Root Cause: Sudden hit on dense rebar or slurry buildup in barrel.
Field Solution: Do NOT force reverse. Back off pressure 10mm, flush slurry with max water flow, then resume slow, steady forward feed.

5

Blade Pinching & Binding in Cut

Root Cause: Concrete slab settlement or internal stress release closing the kerf.
Field Solution: Drive wooden or steel wedges into the cut groove behind the blade to prevent slab weight from pinching the steel core.

6

Core Warping & Motor Overload

Root Cause: Excessive manual down-force or forced feed rate.
Field Solution: Let the weight of the saw do the work. Apply steady, moderate pressure without forcing the blade beyond its cutting capacity.

7

Abnormal Segment Wear / Short Lifespan

Root Cause: Wrong rotation direction or soft bond used on abrasive material.
Field Solution: Verify directional arrow stamped on core matches spindle rotation. Switch to a harder bond formulation for abrasive substrates.

8

Slurry Corrosion & Rusting

Root Cause: Highly alkaline concrete slurry left on steel core post-operation.
Field Solution: Wash tools with clean water immediately after cutting. Dry completely and apply light anti-rust oil before flat storage.

9

Dry Cutting Weld Fatigue

Root Cause: Continuous dry cutting without heat dissipation.
Field Solution: Execute cooling duty cycles: every 15 seconds of dry cutting, pull blade out of cut and let it spin free in air for 3 seconds.

10

Dangerous Micro-Cracks on Weld Line

Root Cause: Side loading, twisting blade in cut, or dropping the tool.
Field Solution: Perform visual crack inspection before every shift. Discard any blade showing core cracks or weld seam separation immediately.
